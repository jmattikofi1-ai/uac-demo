MOT test

What's checked in an MOT?

The MOT confirms that, at the time of the test and without dismantling, your vehicle met the minimum road safety and environmental standards. Search the checklist below.

Body, structure & general items

Free from excessive corrosion or damage in specific areas, with no sharp edges likely to cause injury. Towbars for security and condition, correct operation of the 13-pin electrical socket, speedometer condition and operation, and engine mountings.

Fuel system

No leaks. Security and condition of pipes and hoses. The fuel cap fastens and seals securely, so please have the key available.

Exhaust emissions

The vehicle meets the exhaust emission requirements for its age and fuel type.

Exhaust system

Secure and complete, without serious leaks and not too noisy. Catalyst present where one was fitted as standard.

Seat belts

All fitted belts are checked for type, condition, operation and security. All mandatory seat belts must be in place.

Seats

Driver's seat for adjustment. All seats for security, and seat backs must be able to be secured upright.

Doors

Latch securely when closed. Front doors open from inside and outside, rear doors open from outside. Hinges and catches checked for security and condition.

Mirrors

Minimum number required, plus condition and security.

Load security

Boot or tailgate can be secured in the closed position.

Brakes

Condition (including inappropriate repairs or modifications), operation and performance in an efficiency test. ABS and electronic stability control where fitted, plus warning lights for ABS, ESC, electronic parking brake and brake fluid. Removing wheels and trims is not part of the test.

Tyres & wheels

Condition, security, tyre size and type, and tread depth. Spare tyres aren't inspected. Vehicles first used on or after 1 January 2012 also get a tyre pressure monitoring light check.

Registration plates

Condition, security, colour, and characters correctly formed and spaced.

Lights

Condition and operation, including HID and LED headlamps for cleaning, self-levelling and security. Headlamp aim and main beam warning light.

Bonnet

Securely latches in the closed position.

Wipers & washers

Operate to give the driver a clear view ahead.

Windscreen

Condition, and the driver's view of the road.

Horn

Correct operation and of a suitable type.

Steering & suspension

Condition, steering oil level and operation, with a check for inappropriate repairs or modification, including corrosion to power steering pipes or hoses. Steering lock operation and electronic power steering warning light.

Vehicle identification number

Present on vehicles first used on or after 1 August 1980, with no more than one different VIN displayed (except multistage build vehicles).

Electrical

Visible electrical wiring and battery.

If your vehicle fails

A failed vehicle shouldn't be used on the road, other than to be taken to and from a testing station. We'll undertake all the MOT repairs and offer a free re-test if you have the repairs carried out with us. Contact us for a quote on your failure items.

Good to know

Quick answers

Will servicing with you affect my manufacturer's warranty?

No. Provided your vehicle is serviced and maintained in line with the manufacturer's instructions during the warranty period, you have the right to have the work done by an independent garage without affecting your warranty.

When does my car need its first MOT?

Once it's three years old. There are different rules if you use it as a taxi or it has more than eight passenger seats.

Does an MOT mean my car is in good mechanical condition?

No. It confirms the car met the minimum legal road safety and environmental standards on the day, without dismantling. It doesn't cover the condition of the engine, clutch or gearbox.

How often should I service my car?

To the manufacturer's recommended intervals. For most cars that means an annual service regardless of mileage. Cars with condition-based servicing are serviced when the on-board computer says so.

How often does my air conditioning need servicing?

Manufacturers recommend every two years. A system can lose up to 10 per cent of its gas each year, becoming less efficient and increasing fuel consumption.

Why change the brake fluid?

Not changing brake fluid at regular intervals can be dangerous, and your braking system won't perform at its peak. We have the equipment to do brake fluid changes properly.
